About Trinity

Method and limitations

Trinity answers questions about EU cybersecurity regulation and assesses security policies against NIS2 obligations. It runs on a corpus of ENISA publications — threat landscapes, NIS2 guidance and AI-security frameworks.

Retrieval

Three retrieval architectures index the same corpus: hybrid BM25 + vector with cross-encoder reranking, a Neo4j knowledge graph, and MongoDB Atlas Vector Search. All three are scored by one evaluation harness.

NIS2 assessment

Assessments cover thirteen requirement domains, mirroring the structure ENISA's Technical Implementation Guidance (June 2025) uses to decompose the risk-management measures of Article 21(2). Findings cite a published requirement rather than an internal checklist.

Each finding must quote your document verbatim. Quotes are verified as real substrings of the source; anything the model paraphrases or invents is discarded. A finding claiming coverage without a verifiable quote is reported as not addressed and flagged for manual review.

Measured performance

Assessment quality is benchmarked against hand-labelled ground truth. Current results on a single labelled policy:

MetricResultMeaning
Gap recall100% Deficient requirements never reported as satisfied
Status accuracy69% Verdict matches the labelled expectation
Rationale recall67% Names the specific omission, not just the domain

Limitations

  • Status accuracy of 69% means roughly one finding in three needs correction on review.
  • Ground truth currently covers one labelled document, so the figures above are indicative rather than established.
  • Errors skew toward over-reporting gaps. A control that exists but is worded obliquely may be reported as missing.
  • Scope determination is rule-based and does not decide the judgement-based tests in Article 2(2)(d)–(g).

Trinity does not certify compliance. It surfaces likely gaps for a qualified person to review. See Terms.

Privacy

Last updated 16 August 2026

Documents you submit are not stored. They are held in memory for the duration of the request and discarded when the response is returned.

What happens to a document

  • An uploaded file is written to a temporary directory only so the PDF reader can open it, and that directory is removed before the request completes.
  • The text is split and embedded into an in-memory index that exists for one request. It is never written to a database or vector store.
  • Embeddings and analysis run on a local language model. Document content is not transmitted to any third-party AI service.

Documents fetched by URL

Submitting a URL causes a single server-side HTTP request to it. Only public http and https addresses are retrieved; requests resolving to private, loopback or link-local addresses are refused. Fetched content is handled exactly as an upload — in memory, then discarded.

Scope determination

Scope answers are evaluated in the request and not retained. The determination is rule-based; no data is sent to a language model.

Operational data

Server logs record request paths, timing and errors for reliability purposes. They do not contain document contents or scope answers.

Terms of Use

Last updated 16 August 2026

Trinity is not legal advice and does not certify compliance with NIS2 or any other regulation. Its output is an indicative, automated analysis intended to support review by a qualified person.

Nature of the service

Scope determination applies published criteria from Directive (EU) 2022/2555 to the answers you provide. It cannot decide the judgement-based tests in Article 2(2)(d)–(g), and Member States may extend scope in national transposition. Confirm your position with your national competent authority.

Policy assessment uses automated language analysis. It can misclassify a requirement in either direction: reporting a gap that does not exist, or failing to identify one that does. Findings must be verified against the source document before being acted upon.
